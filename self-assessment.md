# Professional Self-Assessment

Throughout my Bachelor of Science in Computer Science at Southern New Hampshire University, I have built a strong foundation in software development, algorithms, databases, and secure practices. The CS-499 capstone has been the culminating experience, allowing me to enhance previous work and reflect on how these skills prepare me for a career in cybersecurity, with a focus on application security and secure software development.

Completing coursework and developing this ePortfolio has helped me showcase my strengths in several ways. Early courses like CS-250 (Agile processes) taught me collaborative workflows, while CS-300 (data structures) and CS-340 (client/server) strengthened my ability to implement efficient, scalable solutions. CS-499 required me to revisit and improve artifacts, applying professional standards to code, documentation, and communication. This process clarified my values: building reliable, secure systems that protect users and organizations. The ePortfolio demonstrates employability through live demos (e.g., Kanban board), before-and-after code comparisons, and objective narratives—far more compelling than a resume alone.

Specific examples from the program include:  
- **Collaborating in team environments**: The Kanban prototype from CS-250 reflects Agile collaboration, enabling visual task tracking and decision-making for diverse users.  
- **Communicating with stakeholders**: Narratives and the code review video use clear, technical language adapted to technical and non-technical audiences.  
- **Data structures and algorithms**: The AVL-balanced BST enhancement in CS-300 shows trade-off management (space vs. guaranteed O(log n) performance).  
- **Software engineering and database**: The MongoDB enhancement in CS-340 adds aggregation pipelines, sanitization, and role simulation, delivering analytical value and security.  
- **Security**: Defensive programming analysis, input validation, and role checks across enhancements reflect a growing security mindset.

These examples, combined with my active pursuit of CompTIA Security+, position me for entry-level roles such as SOC Analyst or Application Security Engineer. The ePortfolio quantifiably illustrates competence through working prototypes, optimized algorithms, and secure database practices.

My artifacts fit together to form a cohesive portfolio: the Kanban board shows collaborative design, the AVL BST demonstrates algorithmic efficiency, and the MongoDB dashboard illustrates secure, data-driven applications. Together, they highlight end-to-end skills—from process and collaboration to performance and security—preparing me to deliver value in cybersecurity.

This capstone has solidified my readiness to enter the field, combining technical depth with professional communication and a proactive security mindset. I am confident these skills will set me apart in interviews and future roles. Thank you for reviewing my CS-499 ePortfolio.
